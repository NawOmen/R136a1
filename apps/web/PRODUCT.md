## Confirmed scope

The user's own approved storefront image is the visual authority: compact white masthead, warehouse banner, separate blue-icon category controls, five-column desktop product grid and two-column phone grid, request strip and slim footer. Real products, categories, exact ruble prices, images and admission status come from the API. Home remains a catalogue for signed-in users too; account work starts in the dashboard. Preserve Next.js, business routes, account roles and versioned human approval.

Light mode is white, silver-gray and navy; dark mode changes the whole scene and reading surfaces. Preserve the original logo and product colors. Cards have compact text below images. Favorites are local to the browser and marked accordingly. Loading, failure/retry and empty results remain distinct. The default order is popularity based on confirmed net acceptance; name sorting is available.

Category buttons and toggles gently grow on hover without liquid material. Selected key actions and the two guidance heroes use bounded procedural glass shells; text and hit targets remain fixed. The guidance hero actions also grow by 2.5% over 180 ms. No water trail, background parallax or idle decorative animation.

The help and procurement pages retain distinct shells, disclosures and the assistant's protected flow. Their right-hand illustrations are original procedural Three.js objects that turn with the pointer and return to rest. Procurement uses a translucent three-lobed ribbon and studio reflections; help uses a clipboard, parcel and message bubble. Matching light/dark PNG frames appear immediately, with no old-image flash. Rendering is PBR rasterization, not path tracing.

All optional motion has touch, reduced-motion/transparency, forced-color and missing-WebGL behavior. No decorative effect changes commercial data or obscures keyboard focus. Keep production assets, licenses and provenance; local design builds, pixel-diff captures, raw reference collections and browser traces stay outside the PR.
